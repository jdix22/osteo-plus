What is athletic therapy?

Athletic Therapy (AT) is a healthcare profession that blends the latest evidence-based research with hands-on clinical experience. Certified Athletic Therapists are highly trained in injury prevention, assessment, management and treatment of injuries related to physical activity, sports and posture.

Athletic Therapists work with people of all ages and abilities to recover from injuries, prevent future ones, and improve athletic performance and work. At OSTEO+, our Athletic Therapist specializes in the prevention, assessment and management of musculoskeletal injuries, postural imbalances, posture correction, and corrective and supportive taping.

Commonly used for

  • Sports injuries
  • Musculoskeletal injuries
  • Postural imbalances
  • Posture correction
  • Corrective and supportive taping
  • Return-to-sport rehab

What to expect

  1. AssessmentA thorough evaluation of your injury, including your medical history and a physical exam. Your therapist asks how the injury happened and what symptoms you have.
  2. Clinical impressionBased on the assessment, your therapist develops a treatment plan.
  3. TreatmentManual therapy, soft tissue mobilization, stretching and strengthening exercises, plus heat or cold therapy, electrical stimulation and ultrasound to manage pain and promote healing.
  4. RehabilitationA program to help you regain strength and flexibility: exercises for range of motion, reducing swelling and pain, and preventing re-injury.
  5. EducationHow to manage your injury and prevent it from coming back: activities to avoid, recommended exercises, pain management and an expected recovery timeline.
  6. Follow-upFollow-up appointments to monitor your progress and adjust your plan.

How to prepare for treatment

  • Fill out the online intake form before your first visit and create an online profile.
  • If you have diagnostic imaging reports (X-ray, ultrasound, MRI), upload them to your online profile or bring a copy to your appointment.
  • Wear loose, comfortable clothing you can move in easily. Some people bring a gym bag with workout clothes and a water bottle.
  • Avoid eating a big meal 2–3 hours before treatment.
  • Arrive on time so you get the full length of your appointment.
  • After any treatment, hydrate, get plenty of sleep, and keep any exercise gentle: take a walk, stretch or do gentle yoga. Epsom salt baths are also encouraged for muscle relaxation.

Good to know

The number and frequency of sessions varies with the injury or condition. Our Athletic Therapists do not diagnose or practise medicine, nor do we attempt to treat disease. If you are concerned about a medical condition, always consult your physician before starting treatment.

The goal of athletic therapy is to help you recover from injury, manage pain and improve your physical function so you can safely return to your sport or activity as soon as possible.

What to expect after treatment

You may feel tenderness in some muscles or joints, though not everyone does.

If pain-relieving modalities were used you may feel relief right after treatment, then more pain several hours later. This is expected and completely normal.

You may feel more discomfort in the days after treatment. Healing is non-linear and sometimes things get worse before they get better.

If pain persists for several days, gets worse, spreads or changes, let your therapist know.
